## Gates

### 1. Routing goes through the appliance

In Debian, the default route is `via 192.168.127.1 dev wsltap`; `eth0` keeps only its link-scope WSL NAT subnet `172.24.144.0/20`. Byte counters across a 5 MB download: `wsltap` +28 MB RX, `eth0` +0. All IPv4 egress goes through the bridge. PASS.

The RX delta is not background traffic: idle `wsltap` carries ~800 B/s, and three repeated 5 MB downloads gave 36-39 MB RX each. `nstat` across one 5 MB download showed `TcpInSegs` 22663 and `TcpExtTCPDSACKOldSent` 18892, so ~83% of the segments Debian received duplicated data it already had: the TCP sender on the gvproxy side (gVisor netstack) retransmits spuriously at scale. Throughput stays fine (~72 Mbit/s) and the verdict stands, but the stdio bridge carries ~6x the payload. A follow-up `tcpdump` on `wsltap` (5 MB download) confirmed the duplicates are TCP-level resends by the gVisor sender, not frame duplication in the bridge: 6204 of 18963 duplicates carry a newer TSecr than the original segment, which a frame copy cannot produce. SACK and timestamps are negotiated. The trigger is unverified; tracked in TODO (Backlog).

### 2. VPN subnets reachable through the bridge

Both VPN subnets (`10.124.0.0/24`, `10.66.192.0/24`) are reachable from WSL via `wsltap`; TCP DNS queries to `10.124.0.1` and `10.66.192.1` succeed. This is the VPN-bypass property: traffic leaves via the Windows host's network stack instead of being black-holed by the VPN. PASS.

### 3. DNS path

Debian's resolv.conf points at `10.255.255.254` (WSL DNS tunneling), so name resolution goes through the Windows resolver rather than `wsltap`. Expected with DNS tunneling enabled; resolution works.
